So the Egyptians tried something else: water. A water clock, or clepsydra, is basically a stone bowl with a tiny hole near the bottom.

Water drips out at a steady rate, and marks on the inside tell you how much time has passed. One surviving Egyptian water clock is around three thousand four hundred years old.

The ancient Greeks even used water clocks in court, to limit how long people could speak. Talk too long? Your water runs out.

Meanwhile, in Mesopotamia, the Babylonians were obsessed with the number sixty. They did their math in base sixty.

Why sixty? Because it splits evenly into two, three, four, five, six, ten, twelve, fifteen, twenty, and thirty. Super handy for dividing things up.

That ancient habit, passed down through Greek astronomers, is why an hour has sixty minutes and a minute has sixty seconds. Every time you check the clock, you're doing Babylonian math.
