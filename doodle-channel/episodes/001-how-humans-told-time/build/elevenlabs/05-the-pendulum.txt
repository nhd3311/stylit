Then came a bored young man in a church. The story goes that in the 1580s, a student named Galileo Galilei watched a lamp swinging in Pisa Cathedral.

He noticed something weird: big swings and small swings seemed to take the same amount of time.

Historians aren't sure that lamp story really happened. But Galileo really did study pendulums, and realized they could keep time.

In 1656, the Dutch scientist Christiaan Huygens built the first pendulum clock.

The result? Clocks went from losing minutes per day, to losing only seconds. Suddenly, a minute hand was actually worth adding.

For the next two hundred and seventy years, the pendulum ruled the world of timekeeping.
