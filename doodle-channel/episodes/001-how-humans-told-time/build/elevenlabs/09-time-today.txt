And you use atomic clocks every single day, without even knowing it. Every GPS satellite carries atomic clocks on board.

Your phone figures out where you are by measuring how long signals take to arrive from several satellites.

Light travels about thirty centimeters in a billionth of a second. So a timing error of just one millionth of a second could put you around three hundred meters off.

Today, the best experimental optical clocks are so precise, they wouldn't lose a second over the entire age of the universe.

Fun fact: the Earth itself is a sloppy clock. Its spin is slightly irregular, so since 1972, scientists have added leap seconds to keep atomic time in sync with the planet.

But leap seconds cause headaches for computers. So in 2022, the world voted to scrap them by 2035.
