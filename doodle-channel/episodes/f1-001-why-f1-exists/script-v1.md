# F1 Ep. 001: SCRIPT v1 (for approval)

**Working title:** *Why Does F1 Even Exist? The Origin of Formula 1*
**Alt titles:** *How a Newspaper Stunt Became Formula 1* · *The ENTIRE Origin Story of F1 (1894–1950)*
**Format:** narrator (ElevenLabs) + Apex as silent host (white round head, goggles on forehead, orange gloves; see `character/apex.md`) · ~1,050 words · ≈ 7 min (≈ 8 min with pauses)
**Story spine:** F1 exists because of 4 problems people had to solve: *prove the car works → stop killing spectators → make it fair → crown one champion.*

> How to read: the narration is the text read aloud. `▸` = visual idea (becomes a Flow / Lemo-Opuscar scene after approval). `[n]` = source at the bottom. ⭐ = hero shot. ⚠️ = a claim worded carefully because the sources disagree.
>
> 🚫 **IP rule for visuals:** no F1 logo, no team or sponsor logos, no broadcast footage, no F1 theme music. Cars are drawn generic, in historical national colours. Real people = stick figures with one prop.

## COLD OPEN: Silverstone, 1950 (0:00)

May 13th, 1950. An old wartime airfield in the middle of England. [1]
▸ ⭐ Aerial cut-paper view: a former bomber airfield, runways repainted into a race track; crowds as tiny dots.

The King and Queen are in the crowd: the only time a reigning British monarch has ever attended a motor race in Britain. [1]
▸ A royal box with two stick figures in crowns; Apex in a flat cap next to them, starstruck.

On the grid: three Italian cars in blood red. Seventy laps later, an Italian named Giuseppe Farina wins. [1] ⚠️ *(we say "three red Alfas" only for the podium; four entered)*
▸ Red cars (no logos) crossing a chequered line; Farina-figure with goggles waving.

Nobody watching knows it yet, but they've just seen the first ever race of the Formula One World Championship.
▸ Freeze-frame; a giant "1" (added in edit) stamps onto the screen.

But F1 didn't start that day. It started fifty-six years earlier… with a newspaper trying to sell more newspapers.
▸ Apex spins; the scene peels away like old paper to reveal a 1890s newsstand. TITLE CARD.

## CH1: A Newspaper Stunt (≈0:36)

1894. Cars are brand new, noisy and unreliable. Most people think they're a toy for rich weirdos.
▸ Victorian street: horse-drawn carriages, one smoking car chugging past, a horse rolling its eyes.

So a Paris newspaper, *Le Petit Journal*, organises a challenge: drive from Paris to Rouen, about 126 kilometres. [2]
▸ Map line Paris → Rouen; a newspaper-boy Apex shouting with a stack of papers.

But it wasn't a race. The prize was for the vehicle that was safe, easy to handle and cheap to run. The fastest machine, a steam-powered one, didn't even get the top prize. [2]
▸ A steam tractor-car crossing first, puffing; officials shaking their heads; a petrol car politely chugging in.

A year later came Paris–Bordeaux–Paris: almost twelve hundred kilometres, there and back. A man named Émile Levassor got back first, after driving for about two days with almost no rest. [3] ⚠️
▸ Levassor-figure, eyes red, gripping a tiller in the night; a coffee cup rattling.

Why race at all? Because a race is the best advert there is. If your car survives twelve hundred kilometres, people believe it can survive their trip to the market.
▸ Apex as a car salesman pointing at a mud-covered car; buyers nodding.

That's reason number one F1 exists: to prove the machine works.
▸ A checklist card, item 1 ticked: "PROVE IT WORKS" (added in edit).

## CH2: The Race of Death (≈1:32)

Within a few years, cars got much faster. The races didn't change. They still ran on open public roads, between cities, through dust, villages, and crowds standing right at the edge.
▸ ⭐ A dusty country road; huge crowds on both sides; a car blasting through a cloud of dust; Apex ducking behind a tree.

In 1903, more than two hundred cars set off from Paris to Madrid. [4]
▸ Dawn start line, a long queue of cars and riding mechanics.

It turned into a disaster. Crash after crash on the first day. Drivers, mechanics and spectators were killed, including Marcel Renault, one of the brothers who founded Renault. [4] ⚠️ *(death toll differs between sources)*
▸ Somber, minimal: a car-shaped silhouette against a tree, smoke, a dropped goggle. No bodies.

The government stopped the race at Bordeaux. The cars weren't even allowed to drive out under their own power. [4]
▸ Gendarme-figure holding up a hand; a line of cars stopped at a city gate.

After that, the era of racing between cities on open roads was basically over. Racing moved onto closed circuits, loops of road closed to the public. [4]
▸ A map: the long city-to-city line curls up and closes into a loop.

Reason number two: racing needed its own safe place, away from the public.
▸ Checklist item 2 ticked: "A SAFE PLACE".

## CH3: Why Ferrari Is Red (≈2:24)

Around the same time, an American newspaper owner named James Gordon Bennett set up a race between countries, not just car companies. Every nation sent a team. [5]
▸ A cut-paper globe; little teams of Apexes with national flags (generic, simple).

To tell them apart, each country got its own colour. France raced in blue. Germany in white. Italy in red. [5]
▸ Three cars side by side in blue, white and red.

Britain couldn't use red, white or blue: those were taken. And in 1903, the British team raced in Ireland, because racing on public roads was illegal in Britain. So they picked green: a nod to Ireland's shamrock. That's British racing green. [5] ⚠️ *(the popular account; its exact origin is debated)*
▸ ⭐ A green car on an Irish country road, a shamrock shape in the clouds; Apex holding a paint pot.

And that's why, more than a century later, Ferrari is still red. It's Italy's old racing colour.
▸ Smash cut: a modern generic red F1-style car (no logos) next to its 1900s ancestor.

## CH4: The First Grand Prix (≈3:06)

In 1906, the Automobile Club of France held a new kind of race near the town of Le Mans. They called it the Grand Prix: French for "big prize". [6]
▸ French countryside, a closed loop of road; a giant trophy-shaped cloud.

The track was a loop of closed public roads, more than a hundred kilometres around. The race lasted two days, and covered over twelve hundred kilometres. [6]
▸ Map of a triangle-shaped road loop; a two-day calendar flipping.

The winner was Ferenc Szisz, a Hungarian mechanic driving a Renault, with a riding mechanic next to him. Their average speed: about a hundred and one kilometres per hour. On dirt roads. In 1906. [6]
▸ Szisz-figure and a mechanic in goggles, both caked in dust, a mustard sun behind them; Apex as the riding mechanic, hair blown flat.

Grand Prix racing was born. But it was still a mess. Every race had its own rules, and there was no single champion.
▸ A pile of different rulebooks toppling over.

## CH5: Silver Arrows and Hidden Cars (≈3:50)

In the 1930s, Grand Prix racing got a European Championship, and it got political. [7]
▸ A 1930s grandstand with flags; a darker, cobalt palette.

The German government poured money into two teams, Mercedes-Benz and Auto Union, to show off German engineering. Their cars were so dominant and so shiny they became known as the "Silver Arrows". [7]
▸ ⭐ Two silver streamlined cars (no badges) slicing past slower red cars.

There's a famous story that Mercedes scraped the white paint off their car to make it light enough to race. It's a great story. Historians say it's probably not true: the weight limit wasn't even used at that race. [8]
▸ A mechanic sanding paint off a car; then a big vermilion "MYTH?" stamp (added in edit); Apex as a detective with a magnifying glass.

Then the Second World War stopped everything.
▸ Silence; the racetrack overgrown with weeds; a "closed" chain across the gate.

In Italy, Alfa Romeo had a small, brilliant racing car called the 158. To keep it out of the war, the cars were hidden near Milan: legend says in a cheese factory, other accounts say a pig farm. [9] ⚠️
▸ Barn interior: a red car under a pile of wood and hay; a curious pig sniffing it; Apex with a finger to his lips.

Remember that car. It's going to matter.
▸ Close-up on the hidden car's headlight, glinting.

## CH6: The "Formula" (≈4:45)

After the war, the sport's governing body, today called the FIA, wrote one rulebook for top-level racing: a single "formula". [10]
▸ A table of officials in a smoky Paris room, a big book in the middle.

That's literally what the name means. A formula is a set of rules every car must follow. Engine size, weight, design. Formula One was the top one. Lower categories like Formula Two came later. [10]
▸ A recipe card titled with a "1"; ingredients drawn as engine, wheels, weight.

The first version allowed either big 4.5-litre engines, or small 1.5-litre engines with a supercharger. [10]
▸ Two engines on a scale, balanced.

Why bother? Because without one formula, the richest team just builds the biggest monster and wins. A formula makes different machines race on roughly equal terms.
▸ A giant monster-truck car being stopped by a rulebook "wall"; normal cars racing side by side.

Reason number three: make it fair.
▸ Checklist item 3 ticked: "MAKE IT FAIR".

## CH7: One World Champion (≈5:26)

There was still one thing missing: a single champion.
▸ Apex holding an empty trophy, looking around.

In 1949, motorcycles got their own world championship. Car racing didn't want to be left behind. [11]
▸ A motorbike with a trophy zooming past a jealous car.

So in early 1950, an Italian count named Antonio Brivio suggested an idea at the FIA: link the big national Grands Prix into one season, give points to the top finishers, and crown one World Champion of drivers. [11] ⚠️ *(F1 itself says nobody is 100% sure who had the idea, but Brivio usually gets the credit)*
▸ A count in a fine suit sketching dots on a map and connecting them into one line.

The first season had seven races: six in Europe, plus the Indianapolis 500 in America, which counted even though almost none of the European drivers went. [12]
▸ A map: six pins in Europe, one lonely pin in the US with a question mark.

And the first race of all? Silverstone. May 13th, 1950. [1]
▸ Back to the cold-open airfield.

The car that won it was that same little Alfa Romeo 158, the one that had spent the war hidden on a farm. [1][9]
▸ ⭐ The red car bursting out of the hay barn, dissolving into the same car crossing the Silverstone finish line.

Giuseppe Farina won that day, and at the end of the season, he became Formula One's first World Champion. [12]
▸ Farina-figure lifting a trophy; confetti; Apex cheering in the crowd.

## OUTRO: Why F1 Still Exists (≈6:22)

So why does Formula One exist? Because people needed to prove their machines worked, find a safe place to race them, make the fight fair, and crown one champion.
▸ ⭐ The four-item checklist, all ticked, morphing into a 2020s grid of generic cars.

Seventy-six years later, it's the same four reasons. Car makers still use F1 to prove their technology. Teams still fight over the rulebook. And twenty-something drivers still chase one title.
▸ Modern Apex in a race suit walking down a pit lane full of generic cars in team colours.

Not bad for a newspaper stunt.
▸ Apex picks up an 1894 newspaper from the pit lane floor and grins.

If you want the next chapter: how this sport went from a dangerous hobby to a billion-dollar machine, subscribe. See you on the grid.
▸ Apex waving from a car; red button shape (no text).
