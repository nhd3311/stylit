# voice.md: Apex motorsport channel (F1-first)

Read by every `/yt-*` skill (youtube-agent plugin). Copy to `~/.claude/youtube/voice.md`.

## Who I am talking to

One person: a 16–30 year-old who started watching F1 after *Drive to Survive* or a friend's watch party, knows the drivers and the teams, but has no idea where any of it came from. Watches on a phone, often with subtitles on, English as a first or second language. Hates being lectured, loves "wait, WHAT?" facts they can repeat to friends.

## How I actually talk

From ep. 1 (*How a Newspaper Stunt Became Formula 1*):

> If you think Formula One started in nineteen fifty, you're missing the fifty-six years that explain everything you see on the grid today.
> In eighteen ninety-four, the car is barely ten years old. It's loud, it breaks down constantly, and most people think it's a toy for rich eccentrics.
> But there's a catch. The rules say the winner must be a four-seat car, and Levassor's has only two.
> Not bad for a newspaper stunt.

- Present tense for history ("In 1906, the Automobile Club of France runs…"): it puts the viewer in the room.
- Short sentence after a long one. A twist line on its own ("But there's a catch.").
- Story first, number second, meaning third.
- Dry humour, never mocking real victims.

## Words I never use

"Unlock", "game-changer", "dive in", "let's get into it", "buckle up", "insane", "you won't believe", "smash that like button", "legendary" (unless a source calls it that), "Lights out and away we go" (F1 broadcast catchphrase, not ours).

## Words I do use

"Here's the catch." · "Reason number one/two/three/four" · "Remember that car." · "Not bad for…" · "See you on the grid."

## What I will not claim

- **No fact without a source in the script file.** Every number, date and name is checked against at least one source (Wikipedia + a specialist source like Motor Sport, Goodwood, RMG, Formula1.com).
- **When sources disagree, say so** ("legend says…", "the story goes…", "around…", "according to…").
- Never say "the same car" when it was the same model; never say "first ever" unless a source does.
- No invented quotes, no invented crowd sizes, no "fans were furious" without a source.
- No F1, team or sponsor logos, no broadcast footage, no F1 theme music.

## My format

- Long-form 8–12 min, 16:9, English narration (draft: Kokoro `bm_george`; final: ElevenLabs), burned-in English subtitles + an `.srt`.
- Opens with a hook line, then a cold open in the middle of the action, then a title card at ~1:00.
- Chapters in the description; a recurring device per episode (ep. 1: the four-item checklist).
- Host **Apex** (white round head, stick limbs, goggles on forehead, orange gloves) appears in every era, silent; the narrator talks.
- Risograph-editorial look: flat inks, ink outlines, paper grain, national racing colours carry meaning.
- No face cam, no swearing. Ends with a one-line callback + the next episode.
